Abstract

Visual emotion analysis is hampered by the fragmentation of annotation formats across datasets: some use categorical labels, others record valence-arousal coordinates, and others provide probability distributions over emotion categories. We introduce Dimensional Distribution Emotion State (DDES), a unified representation that encodes image emotion as a 28×28 probability heatmap over the valence-arousal (V-A) plane. DDES subsumes and interconverts with two complementary forms — a Dimensional Emotion State (DES, a V-A point) and a Categorical Emotion State (CES, an N-class distribution) — through a small set of closed-form equations. This common embedding space lets a single training loop jointly supervise on heterogeneous datasets and eliminates the need for dataset-specific model heads. Three model variants (DDES-Net, CES-Net, DES-Net) are trained on ArtEmis, D-ViSA, and EmoSet and evaluated on WikiArt-Emotion and EEMO-Bench, achieving competitive or superior performance to prior work across all benchmarks.

Three Emotion Representations

DDES unifies three emotion representations that commonly appear across visual emotion datasets, connected by bidirectional conversion equations:

  • DDES — a 28×28 log-probability heatmap over the valence-arousal plane, used as the canonical form.
  • DES (Dimensional Emotion State) — a single (valence, arousal) point in [−1, 1]².
  • CES (Categorical Emotion State) — an N-class probability distribution over discrete emotion labels.

The conversion equations allow any model to be supervised on any dataset, regardless of annotation format.
